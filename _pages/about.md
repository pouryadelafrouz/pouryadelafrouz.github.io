I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at <a href="https://www.foxchase.org/">Fox Chase Cancer Center</a>, working with <a href="https://comp-epi-lee-lab.org/">Dr. Hayan Lee</a>. I earned my Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering, specializing in Bioinformatics, from the <a href="https://www.uic.edu/">University of Illinois Chicago</a>, under the mentorship of <a href="https://bme.uic.edu/profiles/jie-liang-phd/">Dr. Jie Liang and <a href="https://bme.uic.edu/profiles/ao-ma-phd-2/">Dr. Ao Ma. I also hold an M.S. and a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering.


My research lies at the intersection of computational genomics, biophysics, and epigenomics, focusing on how genome organization and epigenetic regulation influence cellular function, aging, and cancer. I integrate computational modeling, polymer physics, high-throughput sequencing, and statistical analysis to investigate genome architecture across bacterial, nuclear, and mitochondrial systems.

A major focus of my research is three-dimensional (3D) genome organization. I develop and apply computational approaches to reconstruct genome structures and identify the physical and molecular mechanisms governing chromosome folding. My work has explored bacterial chromosome organization in Escherichia coli, chromatin–nuclear lamina interactions, and the relationship between 3D genome architecture and gene regulation.

More recently, my research has expanded toward mitochondrial genomics and epigenetics, investigating mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) organization, genomic instability, and epigenetic modifications. By combining computational modeling with Oxford Nanopore sequencing, I study mitochondrial DNA methylation patterns and their potential associations with aging and cancer.

I also investigate telomere biology, using long-read sequencing and computational approaches to characterize telomere length, chromosome-arm-specific variation, and associated epigenetic features.

My overarching goal is to develop integrative computational approaches that connect genome structure, epigenetic modifications, and genomic variation, advancing our understanding of genome regulation in health, aging, and disease.
